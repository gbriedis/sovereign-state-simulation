# State of Consequence — Brand Foundation

- **Adopted:** 2026-08-22
- **Public name:** *State of Consequence*
- **Category:** A living nation simulation
- **Primary tagline:** Govern what you cannot command.

## Brand idea

**Consequential stewardship.**

The player has sovereign influence without omnipotent control. Policy,
institutions, infrastructure, geography, citizens, companies, and organized
interests interact across decades. The country itself becomes the visible record
of the player's judgment.

## Core promise

> Your decisions become the country.

The emotional reward is earned national transformation: looking back across the
map and seeing how earlier choices changed settlement, production,
infrastructure, institutions, security, knowledge, and living conditions.

## Positioning

For systems-minded strategy players who want meaningful national development,
*State of Consequence* is a map-first living nation simulation where geography,
institutions, policy, and autonomous actors shape decades of change. The player
governs competing interests rather than treating society as an extension of the
cursor, and the nation itself is the progression display.

### Short description

Lead a small sovereign nation through decades of change. Shape policy,
institutions, infrastructure, and national priorities while citizens, companies,
and powerful interests pursue futures of their own. Geography has history,
society has agency, and every decision leaves a mark.

### One-sentence pitch

Shape a nation that has a life of its own, and watch decades of judgment become
visible across the map.

## Audience

### Primary

- Strategy players who seek understandable causes instead of arbitrary bonuses.
- Emergent-simulation players who enjoy autonomous actors and unplanned stories.
- Long-horizon builders who want national stewardship rather than direct city
  micromanagement.

### Secondary and future

- Geography, procedural-generation, and simulation-engine enthusiasts are a
  valuable early community, but the physical-world engine is evidence for the
  player fantasy rather than the commercial center of it.
- Persistent-world strategy players become important when multiplayer depth can
  be demonstrated without weakening each personal nation.

## Messaging pillars

### 1. A nation with a life of its own

Citizens, companies, institutions, and major interests have their own knowledge,
incentives, power, and constraints. Leadership works through policy, resources,
negotiation, regulation, and opposition.

### 2. Progress written across the map

The country is the progression screen. Roads, production, settlement, energy,
institutions, knowledge, and living conditions show what changed; invented ruler
levels and generic upgrade tiers do not.

### 3. A world with reasons

Geography is history-bearing and causal rather than decorative. Terrain,
resources, water, climate, and engineering conditions establish meaningful
possibilities and constraints for the nation that later develops there.

### 4. Decisions with an afterlife

Choices propagate through systems, meet actors with agency, and compound across
decades. Outcomes may be delayed, redirected, resisted, or unexpectedly
successful.

## Voice

The voice is grounded, intelligent, patient, materially specific, humane, and
quietly epic. It explains cause and effect clearly, then lets the consequence
carry the drama.

### Prefer

- Concrete verbs and measurable change.
- Tradeoffs, reactions, inherited conditions, and long time horizons.
- Confidence without pretending every outcome is predictable.
- Short evocative lines supported by specific systems.

### Avoid

- Conquest-first, triumphalist, or ruler-power fantasies.
- Claims of perfectly simulating reality.
- Empty superlatives such as “ultimate,” “limitless,” or “most realistic ever.”
- Administrative jargon that makes the game sound like government software.
- Leading with MMO, Web3, technical architecture, or geological detail before
  the player fantasy is established.

### Approved lines

- Govern what you cannot command.
- Your decisions become the country.
- Every landscape has a past. Every policy has an afterlife.
- The river was here before the border.
- A world with reasons.
- Shape a nation that has a life of its own.
- Decades of decisions, written across the map.

## Visual identity direction

### Character

Cartographic, institutional, human, and history-bearing. The identity should feel
precise and serious without becoming sterile, militaristic, or pseudo-imperial.

### Foundational palette

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary dark | Deep ink | `#0C141B` |
| Primary light | Limestone | `#E8E1D4` |
| Consequence accent | Oxidized copper | `#B5623A` |
| World accent | Survey green | `#657A67` |

These are brand-direction colors, not a final accessible interface palette.
Player-interface combinations require contrast and usability testing.

### Graphic language

- Use terrain contours, watersheds, boundaries, networks, and accumulated map
  change as primary motifs.
- Explore a mark in which physical contour lines meet or redirect an
  administrative boundary.
- Pair an editorial serif display face with a highly legible cartographic sans
  for supporting information.
- Show the same place changing across time whenever possible.

Avoid generic globes, flags, crowns, eagles, chess pieces, military stencils,
and ornamental “strategy game gold.”

## Development-story architecture

- **Before the Border** — physical-world generation and the causal history of
  land before politics.
- **The Country Reacts** — autonomous actors, institutions, incentives, and
  second-order effects.
- **Decades Later** — visible national transformation and before/after stories.

Each technical story should reconnect to a player-facing consequence. Geology is
the credibility engine beneath the game, not the headline genre.

## Name usage

- Use *State of Consequence* as the public product name.
- Use **State of Consequence** without italics in interfaces, metadata, and other
  contexts where styling is unavailable.
- Use “a living nation simulation” as the default category descriptor.
- Retire “Sovereign-State Simulation” as the public working title.
- Use `state-of-consequence-client` and `state-of-consequence-world` for future
  first-party Cargo package identifiers so technical metadata follows the
  accepted identity.

## Claims discipline

Current communication must distinguish demonstrated systems from long-term
intent. The natural-world foundation is in development; national simulation and
persistent multiplayer are product direction rather than implemented features.
Web3 is optional and non-foundational and should not appear in core brand
messaging.

## Pre-publication gates

Before a public reveal or commercial commitment:

1. Complete professional trademark clearance in intended markets and relevant
   software and entertainment classes.
2. Secure the chosen domain and priority community handles.
3. Test pronunciation, meaning, and unintended associations in priority
   languages.
4. Produce and test the wordmark, compact mark, store capsule treatment, and
   accessible digital palette.
5. Validate the promise with target players using actual prototype footage and
   language rather than concept statements alone.
